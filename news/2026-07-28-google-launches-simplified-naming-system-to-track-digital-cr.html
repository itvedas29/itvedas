Google Launches Simplified Naming System to Track Digital Criminals

Google introduces easier-to-remember names for tracking dangerous hackers and cyber criminals across the internet.

Google Changes How It Names Cyber Criminals

Search giant Google has rolled out a fresh approach for identifying and discussing dangerous hacking groups operating worldwide. Rather than relying on complex technical codes or lengthy classification systems, the company is switching to a straightforward two-word naming format designed to make criminal organizations easier to discuss in public conversations and news reports.

The new system pairs a catchy, recognizable term with a descriptive category word. Think of it like how weather forecasters name hurricanes — instead of referring to a storm as "System 2024-Atlantic-7," they call it "Hurricane Milton," making it instantly memorable and shareable. Google's approach works similarly for the digital threat landscape.

Why This Change Matters in Cybersecurity

Previously, security researchers and companies used inconsistent labeling when discussing hacking groups. One organization might call a criminal gang "APT-28" while another refers to the same group by a completely different name. This confusion made it harder for businesses and governments to understand which threats were which and share information effectively.

The new standardized naming system creates a common language. When a news article reports on a specific hacking group, security teams everywhere will immediately know which criminals are being discussed. This shared vocabulary strengthens communication across the entire cybersecurity industry.

A unified naming system acts like a universal translator for the security world — everyone talks about the same threat using identical terminology.

Google's move signals that major technology companies recognize a pressing need for clarity. As cyber attacks grow more sophisticated and frequent, the ability to quickly identify and discuss threats becomes crucial for defense and prevention.

What This Means for You

If you work in IT security or manage company networks, this development simplifies threat research. Instead of cross-referencing multiple databases to confirm whether different names refer to the same criminal group, you can now rely on Google's standardized labels. This saves time and reduces confusion during incident response situations.

For business leaders and executives, clearer threat communication means better decision-making. When your security team discusses an emerging threat using a consistent, widely-recognized name, you can more easily understand the risk level and take appropriate action.

Regular internet users benefit too, though indirectly. As security professionals communicate more clearly about threats, defense systems improve, ultimately protecting consumers from attacks.

What You Should Do Now

  • Stay informed: Follow official Google security announcements to learn the new naming conventions as they're introduced
  • Update your references: If you maintain threat intelligence documentation, begin noting the new standardized names alongside any legacy terminology
  • Train your team: Ensure security staff understand and use the new naming system in internal discussions and incident reports
  • Watch for adoption: Other major security companies will likely follow Google's lead, so expect industry-wide standardization within months

Google's new threat actor naming system represents a practical step toward making cybersecurity communication clearer and more effective across organizations worldwide, ultimately creating stronger defenses against digital criminals.
