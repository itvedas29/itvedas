JetBrains TeamCity Left Open to Hackers, Cloud Credentials Stolen

Unpatched software flaw allowed criminals to access JetBrains systems and steal Amazon cloud login details.

Major Software Maker Falls Victim to Preventable Attack

JetBrains, a company that creates popular programming tools used by millions of developers worldwide, suffered a serious security breach. Attackers exploited an unfixed flaw in TeamCity, one of their main products, to break into company systems and make off with sensitive cloud credentials for Amazon Web Services (AWS). The incident highlights how even well-known technology firms can become vulnerable when they fail to patch known security holes.

How the Attack Worked

Think of a security vulnerability like a broken lock on a door. JetBrains had a broken lock on TeamCity, and even though the company knew about it, they hadn't replaced it yet. Attackers found that open door and walked right in. They were able to run their own malicious instructions on the system without needing a password, giving them deep access to internal networks. Once inside, they discovered and extracted AWS credentials—essentially master keys to JetBrains' cloud storage and services.

These credentials are like having someone's house keys. With them, an attacker can access, view, or even delete sensitive information stored in the cloud.

Why This Matters for the Tech Industry

JetBrains isn't a small startup. Their tools help developers at thousands of companies build software. If hackers gained access to JetBrains' systems, they potentially gained a window into projects their clients are working on. This creates a ripple effect—the breach doesn't just affect JetBrains employees, but anyone using their products and platforms.

The core issue: A known, fixable problem was left unpatched. This is like knowing your house has a broken window but choosing not to fix it for weeks.

What Companies Should Do Right Now

  • Apply updates immediately: If your organization uses TeamCity, install any available security patches without delay
  • Review access logs: Check who accessed your systems and when to spot any suspicious activity
  • Rotate cloud credentials: Change any AWS or similar cloud provider passwords and access keys
  • Monitor for unauthorized activity: Watch for unusual data access or transfers that might indicate ongoing compromise

Lessons for Organizations Everywhere

This incident serves as a warning about patch management—the process of regularly updating software. Many companies delay updates because they worry about disrupting their operations. However, leaving known security holes open creates far bigger risks. A few hours of planned downtime for updates is much better than dealing with a full-scale breach.

For individuals and smaller companies, the lesson is simpler: when your software offers you an update, don't ignore it. Your devices—computers, phones, routers—automatically patch themselves with good reason.

What This Means For You

If you're a developer or work at a tech company using JetBrains tools, your organization should treat this breach seriously. Talk to your IT team about their response plan. If you store important projects on TeamCity, now is the time to ensure your credentials are secure and your systems are up to date.

The JetBrains breach demonstrates that security isn't something you can ignore—it requires constant attention and swift action when problems emerge.
