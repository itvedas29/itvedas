Runtime Security Startup Oligo Lands $60M Investment to Protect AI Systems

Oligo secures major funding to expand defenses against threats targeting AI applications during execution.

A Major Bet on AI Protection

Oligo, a cybersecurity company focused on protecting artificial intelligence systems while they're running, just announced it has raised $60 million in fresh funding. The startup plans to use this money to build better security tools and reach more customers around the world.

Think of runtime security like having a security guard watching a store during business hours. Just as thieves might try to break in while the store is open, hackers attempt to attack AI systems while they're actively working and making decisions. Oligo's technology catches these threats in real-time, before they can cause damage.

Why This Matters Now

Artificial intelligence is moving from research labs into everyday business. Companies now use AI to approve loans, detect fraud, recommend products, and manage infrastructure. When these systems get attacked or manipulated, real people face real consequences—wrong medical diagnoses, rejected job applications, or stolen data.

Traditional security focuses on protecting data sitting in storage, like guarding a vault. But AI systems need protection while actually functioning. A hacker might slip a hidden instruction into an AI model that makes it behave unexpectedly. This could happen silently, without anyone noticing.

Oligo's funding announcement signals that major investors now believe runtime AI security is critical. This money allows the company to hire more engineers, build stronger detection tools, and help more organizations defend their AI investments.

The Bigger Picture for Businesses

As companies rush to deploy AI in customer-facing services, security becomes a competitive advantage. Organizations that can confidently say their AI systems are protected will win customer trust. Those that experience AI-related breaches will face lawsuits, regulatory fines, and damaged reputations.

This funding also reflects broader market trends. Investors see that businesses need specialized tools for AI security—not just general cybersecurity solutions. Just like we developed unique defenses for cloud computing and mobile devices, we now need dedicated protections for artificial intelligence.

What This Means for You

  • If you work in technology: Expect your company to invest in runtime security tools within the next year or two. Learning about AI security could make you more valuable to employers.
  • If you're a business leader: Add AI security to your risk assessment. Ask vendors what happens if someone attacks your AI systems during operation.
  • If you're a consumer: Know that companies using your data for AI decisions should be implementing these protections. You have the right to ask how they secure AI systems.

What You Can Do

Start conversations about AI security within your organization. If your company uses artificial intelligence, ask your IT department what defenses they have in place specifically for AI threats. Don't assume that traditional security tools handle AI risks—they typically don't.

For business buyers, this funding round shows that runtime security companies are now well-funded and stable. If you've been considering these tools, this is a good time to evaluate them seriously.

The bottom line: AI is too important to leave unguarded, and Oligo's $60 million injection shows the industry finally agrees.
