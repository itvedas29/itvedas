Fake AI Tool on Chrome Store Caught Stealing User Data and Search History

Cybercriminals disguised malicious software as Perplexity AI, secretly monitoring millions of users' online activity.

A Trojan Horse in Your Browser

Security researchers recently uncovered a dangerous imposter lurking in Google's Chrome Web Store. Someone created a fraudulent extension pretending to be Perplexity, a popular artificial intelligence search tool. However, instead of helping users find answers, this fake version was quietly watching everything they typed, clicked, and searched for online. Think of it like installing what you thought was a helpful calculator app, only to discover it was secretly recording every number you entered and selling that information.

This discovery reveals how easily criminals can exploit the trust people place in legitimate technology brands. The fake extension mimicked Perplexity's appearance and functionality so convincingly that thousands of users downloaded it without suspicion. Once installed, the malicious code began harvesting sensitive browsing data, essentially giving hackers a window into victims' private digital lives.

Why This Matters for Digital Security

This incident exposes a critical vulnerability in how we interact with online tools and services. Most people assume that anything available in official app stores has been vetted for safety. Unfortunately, that assumption is increasingly dangerous. The Chrome Web Store, despite Google's oversight, can still harbor threats when criminals are sophisticated enough.

The stakes are particularly high because browsing data reveals incredibly personal information. Your search history shows what you're interested in, what you're worried about, and sometimes reveals confidential work matters or health concerns. When bad actors collect this data, they can:

  • Build detailed profiles for targeted scams and phishing attacks
  • Sell information to competitors or advertisers
  • Identify victims for blackmail or identity theft
  • Intercept login credentials and financial information

Protecting Yourself Moving Forward

Start by auditing your current browser extensions right now. Open your Chrome settings, navigate to extensions, and honestly evaluate each one. Ask yourself: Do I still use this? Does it come from a reputable company? If you're uncertain, remove it.

When installing new extensions in the future, follow these practical steps:

  • Verify the publisher's official website before downloading anything
  • Check user reviews carefully, looking for red flags about data collection
  • Read the permission requests—does a calculator really need access to all your browsing data?
  • Stick with well-known tools from established companies
  • Enable two-factor authentication on important accounts as an extra security layer
Remember: if you're not paying for the product, you might be the product being sold.

Consider using password managers and enabling browser security features that warn you about suspicious websites. Keep your operating system and Chrome itself updated, as security patches fix vulnerabilities that criminals exploit.

The Bigger Picture

This incident isn't just about one fake extension—it's a warning sign that the internet's trust infrastructure is cracking. As artificial intelligence tools become more popular and integrated into daily life, criminals will increasingly create counterfeit versions to steal from users who want to benefit from this technology.

The responsibility doesn't fall entirely on individual users. Tech companies, app store operators, and security researchers must collaborate to identify threats faster and remove them more aggressively. However, your personal vigilance remains your strongest defense against these digital predators.

Stay skeptical, stay informed, and verify before you install anything new on your devices.
