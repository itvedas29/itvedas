Malicious Browser Extensions Target AI Users With Session-Stealing Malware

Criminals are using fake Chrome extensions to harvest Claude AI login credentials and drain user accounts.

Malicious Extensions Are Targeting AI Users

Security researchers have discovered a troubling trend: criminals are distributing harmful software through the Chrome Web Store designed to steal login information from people using Claude, Anthropic's popular artificial intelligence assistant. Once installed, these extensions silently capture active user sessions—essentially copying the digital "key" that keeps you logged into your account. This allows hackers to log in as you without needing your actual password.

The malware operates like a digital pickpocket. Think of your login session as a movie theater ticket that proves you already bought admission. A thief who steals your ticket can walk right in without buying their own. Similarly, these extensions don't need to guess your password because they're stealing the proof that you're already authenticated.

Why This Matters for Your Security

This isn't just about one service being compromised. These same malicious extensions are also grabbing other sensitive data from your computer—including cryptocurrency wallet information and passwords stored in your browser. Anthropic has already warned some Claude users that their accounts were accessed by unauthorized people, meaning attackers are actively using stolen sessions to consume expensive API credits and potentially access confidential conversations.

The situation reveals a larger security problem: browser extensions have deep access to everything you do online. An extension can see what websites you visit, what you type, and any information you submit. Most people never review which extensions they've installed or what permissions they have.

What You Should Do Right Now

  • Review your extensions: Open Chrome's settings and look at every installed extension. Remove anything you don't recognize or haven't used recently. Ask yourself: do I actually need this?
  • Change your Claude password: If you use Claude, update your password immediately. This invalidates any stolen sessions, locking attackers out of your account even if they captured your login information.
  • Check your API usage: If you have a Claude account, log in and review your recent activity and token usage. Look for unusual access patterns or accounts you don't recognize using your credits.
  • Be cautious with new extensions: Before installing any extension, check its reviews and see how many people use it. Look up its creator's reputation. Genuine tools usually have thousands of reviews and clear developer information.
  • Use a password manager: Consider switching to a dedicated password manager instead of letting your browser store passwords. This adds an extra layer of security.
  • Enable two-factor authentication: Protect important accounts like Claude, email, and cryptocurrency platforms with two-factor authentication, which requires a second verification step even if someone has your password.

What This Means Going Forward

The real issue here is that we've built a system where hundreds of thousands of extensions have direct access to our most sensitive digital activities, with minimal oversight.

This incident exposes how vulnerable our daily tools can be. Chrome's extension marketplace has limited security screening, making it relatively easy for criminals to hide malware in plain sight. As more people use AI assistants for work and store valuable information in cloud accounts, these tools become increasingly attractive targets for thieves.

Protect yourself by treating browser extensions like security vulnerabilities waiting to happen—only install what you truly need, from sources you trust.
