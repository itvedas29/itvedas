Palo Alto Networks Expands Beyond Security with Embrace Platform Purchase

Major cybersecurity firm continues building observability tools through second major acquisition this year.

Palo Alto Networks Makes Second Major Move Into Application Monitoring

Cybersecurity giant Palo Alto Networks has announced plans to purchase Embrace, a software company specializing in mobile and application performance monitoring. This deal represents the second significant expansion effort by the company in recent months, following a similar acquisition earlier this year. The move signals an aggressive strategy to grow beyond traditional security software into the broader world of system visibility and performance tracking.

Embrace focuses on helping developers understand how their applications behave in the real world. Think of it like having security cameras throughout a building—but instead of watching for intruders, you're watching to see if things are running smoothly, if users are experiencing delays, or if something unexpected is happening with your software. Embrace specializes in mobile apps, helping companies see what's happening on users' phones and tablets.

What This Means for the Industry

Palo Alto Networks is essentially saying that security alone isn't enough anymore. Modern companies need to understand their entire technology ecosystem—not just protect it. This is part of a larger industry trend where security companies are becoming broader technology partners.

By purchasing Embrace, Palo Alto gains immediate access to tools and expertise in a different market segment. The company is essentially building a toolkit that covers multiple angles:

  • Traditional security (what they've always done)
  • Network monitoring and performance (added earlier this year with Chronosphere)
  • Application-level visibility (now with Embrace)

When companies buy multiple related tools from one vendor, it creates what's called a "platform." Instead of juggling five different software subscriptions from five different companies, customers can use one integrated system. This makes life simpler for IT teams and creates stronger customer loyalty.

Why This Matters to Your Organization

If your company uses Palo Alto Networks products, you should pay attention. Over the next year or two, you'll likely see new features and integrations that combine security monitoring with application performance tracking. This could be genuinely useful—one dashboard showing everything—or it could become overwhelming with too many features bundled together.

For companies still deciding which security vendor to use, this signals that Palo Alto is investing heavily in staying relevant and competitive. They're not resting on their security reputation; they're building something bigger. That kind of investment typically means better products down the road, but it also means higher prices as they integrate their acquisitions.

If you manage mobile applications or care about how your software performs for end users, take note: Embrace's tools are about to become part of a much larger ecosystem. This could expand what's possible, or it could mean changes to how you currently use Embrace.

What You Can Do

  • If you're a current Embrace customer, contact your account manager to understand the transition timeline and integration plans
  • If you're evaluating security and monitoring tools, consider how well an all-in-one platform from Palo Alto might work versus best-of-breed solutions from multiple vendors
  • Keep an eye on Palo Alto's roadmap for 2024 and beyond to see how these pieces fit together

Palo Alto Networks is betting that companies want one trusted partner for multiple technology needs rather than managing separate vendors.
