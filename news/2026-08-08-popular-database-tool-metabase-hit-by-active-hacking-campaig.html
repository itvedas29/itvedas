Popular Database Tool Metabase Hit by Active Hacking Campaign Targeting Customer Records

Hackers are exploiting a newly discovered security flaw in Metabase to steal customer data from businesses worldwide.

A Critical Vulnerability Puts Business Data at Risk

Security researchers have uncovered an active hacking campaign targeting users of Metabase, a widely-used tool that helps businesses organize and analyze their data. Attackers are leveraging a previously unknown security weakness—essentially a hidden doorway in the software—to break into customer databases and steal sensitive information. The vulnerability allows hackers to inject malicious commands directly into the system, giving them unauthorized access to whatever data the tool can reach.

Unlike vulnerabilities that are discovered and then publicly announced, this weakness was already being exploited in real-world attacks before the security community became aware of it. This gives hackers a significant advantage, as many organizations may not have implemented protections yet.

What This Means

Think of Metabase as a filing system for digital information. It sits between a company's data and the people who need to use it. When a security flaw exists in this filing system, it's like leaving the filing cabinet unlocked—anyone who finds it can walk in and take whatever documents they want.

This particular flaw is especially dangerous because it doesn't require the attacker to have legitimate access to the system. They can exploit it from the outside, without needing a password or user account. The attack method is called SQL injection, which works by slipping harmful code into normal requests, similar to how someone might slip a fake instruction into a stack of legitimate paperwork to manipulate the outcome.

Companies relying on Metabase for business intelligence—including financial records, customer information, sales data, and proprietary research—could potentially have this information stolen.

Why You Should Care

If you work for or do business with any organization using Metabase, your data could be at risk. This includes:

  • Customer names and contact information
  • Financial and billing records
  • Employee information
  • Proprietary business strategies and plans
  • Intellectual property and research data

Even if you don't directly use Metabase, your information might be stored in a system that does. The broader concern is that attackers are actively hunting for vulnerable systems right now, making this an urgent threat rather than a theoretical problem.

What You Can Do

If you manage or work in IT for an organization using Metabase:

  • Update immediately: Check with your software provider for a security patch and apply it as soon as possible
  • Review logs: Examine your system's activity records to see if anyone has exploited this weakness already
  • Notify your team: Make sure everyone knows about the vulnerability and the importance of the update
  • Monitor for compromises: Watch for unusual access patterns or data transfers that could indicate a breach
  • Check credentials: If a breach occurred, consider changing passwords and monitoring accounts for suspicious activity
Organizations should treat this situation with urgency—waiting even a few days to patch could result in data theft.

For regular users and customers, stay alert for communications from your service providers about this issue and follow their guidance on any protective steps you should take.

This incident demonstrates why keeping software updated and maintaining strong security practices isn't optional—it's essential protection for your data.
