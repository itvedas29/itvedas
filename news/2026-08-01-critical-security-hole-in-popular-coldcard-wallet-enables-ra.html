Critical Security Hole in Popular Coldcard Wallet Enables Rapid Bitcoin Heist Worth $70 Million

A vulnerability in Coldcard hardware wallets allowed attackers to steal $70M in Bitcoin within minutes, raising urgent questions about device security.

A Major Security Breach Shakes the Hardware Wallet Industry

Security researchers have uncovered a serious vulnerability affecting Coldcard, one of the most popular hardware wallets used to store cryptocurrency. The flaw enabled attackers to bypass security protections and steal approximately $70 million worth of Bitcoin in just 41 minutes. This incident highlights a critical risk that many cryptocurrency holders may not have realized existed in their supposedly "safe" storage devices.

Hardware wallets are physical devices designed to keep digital currencies secure, similar to how a safe deposit box protects valuables in a bank. Coldcard has earned a strong reputation among Bitcoin enthusiasts for being exceptionally secure. However, this recent discovery reveals that even well-regarded security products can have hidden weaknesses waiting to be exploited.

Understanding the Technical Problem

The vulnerability operates like a hidden door in a bank vault that skilled thieves learned to open. The flaw allowed attackers to access the device's private keys—the digital codes that prove ownership and authorize transfers of cryptocurrency. Without these keys, no one should be able to move funds from the wallet. Once cybercriminals obtained the keys, they could transfer the entire balance to accounts they controlled, and the process happened so quickly that the owner had almost no time to notice or stop it.

What This Means for Digital Security

This incident demonstrates that physical security devices are not invincible. Just as a combination lock can be picked by someone with specialized tools and knowledge, digital security devices can be compromised through unforeseen technical flaws. The speed of the theft—mere minutes—shows that once access is gained, modern systems can move large amounts of value almost instantly with minimal opportunity for intervention.

The discovery also raises questions about how thoroughly hardware wallet manufacturers test their products before release. Security experts typically find vulnerabilities through rigorous testing, but no system can be guaranteed 100% secure against all future threats.

Why You Should Pay Attention

  • Direct risk: If you own a Coldcard wallet, you may be exposed to this vulnerability until patches are released and applied
  • Broader concern: This proves that trusted security products can have serious flaws, even after years of use
  • Trust erosion: People who invested in hardware wallets specifically for maximum protection now face uncomfortable questions about whether they made the right choice

What Cryptocurrency Holders Should Do Now

If you use a Coldcard wallet or any hardware wallet, take these steps immediately:

  • Check the manufacturer's website for security updates and patches
  • Install any available firmware updates right away
  • Monitor your account activity closely for any unauthorized transfers
  • Consider moving funds to alternative storage if you cannot update immediately
  • Enable all available security features, including multi-signature requirements if offered
The cryptocurrency community must demand that manufacturers be more transparent about security testing and more rapid in responding to discovered vulnerabilities.

This incident serves as a reminder that security is not a destination but an ongoing process requiring constant vigilance, regular updates, and honest acknowledgment of risks.
