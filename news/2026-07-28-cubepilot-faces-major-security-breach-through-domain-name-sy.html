CubePilot Faces Major Security Breach Through Domain Name System Attack

Attackers hijacked DNS records for drone software maker, redirecting users to malicious sites and stealing data.

Attackers Redirect CubePilot Users Through Compromised Internet Address System

CubePilot, a company that develops software controlling unmanned aircraft systems, recently discovered that criminals had taken control of its domain name system (DNS) infrastructure. This breach allowed attackers to intercept and redirect network traffic intended for the company's legitimate websites and services toward fraudulent destinations under their control.

Think of DNS like a phone directory for the internet. When you type a web address into your browser, your computer asks a DNS server "where should I go?" The server responds with an IP address—essentially the location of the actual computer hosting that website. In this attack, someone gained unauthorized access to CubePilot's DNS records and changed the answers. Users who tried visiting CubePilot's sites were unknowingly sent to fake websites controlled by the attackers instead.

What This Means

This type of attack, called DNS hijacking, represents a fundamental threat to internet security. It's particularly dangerous because victims may not realize they've been redirected. The website they see looks authentic, browsers don't show obvious warning signs, and users willingly enter login credentials, download files, or share sensitive information with attackers.

For CubePilot specifically, this means anyone attempting to access their services during the attack window may have experienced one or more of these problems:

  • Exposure of login usernames and passwords
  • Download of compromised or malicious software versions
  • Installation of spyware or other malicious code on their systems
  • Theft of proprietary drone software or configuration files

The timing makes this particularly concerning. Drone technology is increasingly used in industries ranging from agriculture and construction to emergency response and military applications. Compromised software could have cascading effects across multiple sectors.

Why You Should Care

If you work with CubePilot products or operate drones using their software, your systems may have been affected. Even if you weren't directly impacted, this incident illustrates a growing vulnerability in internet infrastructure that affects all of us.

DNS hijacking works at a layer of the internet so fundamental that traditional security tools often can't detect it.

Most companies focus on protecting their servers and websites themselves. But if attackers control the DNS records pointing to those servers, it doesn't matter how secure the actual website is—users never reach it. This is why cybersecurity experts consider DNS attacks particularly insidious.

What You Can Do

If you use CubePilot products, take these steps immediately:

  • Change your passwords for any CubePilot accounts using a different device
  • Scan your computer and devices for malware using reputable security software
  • Check your systems for unauthorized access or unusual activity
  • Contact CubePilot directly through a phone number from their official documentation (not from any website) to confirm you're receiving legitimate guidance
  • Review any software you downloaded recently from their services for authenticity

For everyone else, this serves as a reminder to verify website authenticity before entering sensitive information and to keep security software updated on all devices.

The CubePilot breach demonstrates that even specialized software companies require robust security practices across their entire digital infrastructure, not just their visible products.
