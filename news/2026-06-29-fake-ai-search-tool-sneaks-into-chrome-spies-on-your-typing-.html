Fake AI Search Tool Sneaks Into Chrome, Spies on Your Typing and Searches

Cybersecurity researchers discovered a fraudulent browser extension impersonating a popular AI chatbot that secretly monitored user activity.

A Fake Tool Caught Red-Handed

Security researchers recently uncovered a dangerous imposter lurking in Google's Chrome browser. Someone created a fake extension pretending to be Perplexity, a well-known artificial intelligence search tool, but the extension had a sinister purpose: it was watching everything users typed into their search bars and address fields. This discovery represents a growing threat where criminals disguise harmful software as legitimate applications to steal sensitive information.

Think of browser extensions like apps you install on your phone. Just as a fake banking app could steal your login details, this fake browser tool was designed to capture every keystroke users entered—searches, website addresses, passwords, and personal information. The extension was actively intercepting data before it could reach its intended destination.

Why This Matters for Your Digital Safety

When you type something into your browser, you're usually sharing it with the website you're visiting. But this malicious extension was acting like an eavesdropper in a coffee shop, capturing conversations it had no right to hear. Everything users searched for—medical conditions, financial information, private messages—potentially ended up in the wrong hands.

The concerning part is how the extension disguised itself. By mimicking Perplexity's legitimate name and appearance, it exploited user trust. People downloading it thought they were getting the real tool. This technique, called "impersonation," is becoming increasingly common because it works—many users don't carefully verify what they're installing.

Browser extensions have access to almost everything you do online, making them prime targets for cybercriminals seeking to steal data at scale.

What This Reveals About Extension Security

Browser extensions are powerful tools that can enhance your internet experience, but they're also dangerous if you don't trust who created them. Unlike regular websites, extensions can see your browsing history, monitor searches, and access data from every site you visit. Granting this access to a malicious extension is like giving someone a key to your entire digital house.

While Google's Chrome Web Store has security measures in place, determined criminals still find ways to sneak malicious extensions through. This breach shows that the responsibility for staying safe partly falls on users themselves.

Protecting Yourself Moving Forward

  • Verify before installing: Always check the official website of any tool before downloading its extension. Visit the company's main site and look for their official Chrome extension link.
  • Read reviews carefully: Legitimate extensions have numerous reviews from established users. Fake ones often have few reviews or suspiciously perfect ratings.
  • Check permissions: Before installing, review what access the extension requests. If a simple search tool asks for permission to access all your data, that's a red flag.
  • Remove suspicious extensions: Go to your Chrome settings, find extensions, and delete anything you don't recognize or actively use.
  • Keep Chrome updated: Updates include security patches that help block known threats.
  • Use strong, unique passwords: If your information is compromised, unique passwords limit damage to just one account.

The discovery of this fake Perplexity extension serves as a reminder that threats don't always announce themselves loudly—sometimes danger wears a familiar disguise, making vigilance your best defense.
