Mac Users Face New Browser Hijacking Threat as AmnesiaStealer Malware Emerges

A newly discovered macOS malware can take remote control of web browsers and steal user sessions, putting Apple computer owners at risk.

Security researchers have uncovered a dangerous piece of malware targeting Apple computer users. Called AmnesiaStealer, this threat can secretly take control of your web browser and access your online accounts without your knowledge. The discovery comes as the Threema messaging platform faced multiple coordinated cyberattacks that knocked the service offline earlier this week.

How the Attack Works

Think of your browser like the front door to your digital house. Normally, only you have the key to enter. This new malware essentially steals your key and lets hackers stand at your door, pretending to be you. Once installed on a Mac, AmnesiaStealer can monitor your browsing sessions and take remote control of them.

What makes this threat particularly dangerous is that it works silently in the background. You may not notice anything unusual while hackers access your email, social media, banking apps, or any other website where you are logged in. They can see everything you see, click where you click, and access information you thought was private.

The malware operates by intercepting the connection between your browser and the websites you visit. It essentially becomes a middle person in that conversation, able to see and manipulate everything that passes through.

What This Means

This discovery signals a troubling shift in how malware targets Mac users. For years, Apple computer owners believed their systems were safer than Windows machines. While Macs do have built-in security features, they are increasingly attractive targets to criminals because more people assume they are protected.

The connection to recent attacks on Threema—a service specifically designed to keep communications private—shows that security-conscious users are being actively targeted. If hackers are willing to launch major attacks on encrypted messaging platforms, they are clearly motivated and well-organized.

Why You Should Care

If you use a Mac, this matters because:

  • Your accounts are exposed: Any website where you stay logged in becomes vulnerable to unauthorized access
  • Financial information is at risk: Banking, payment apps, and shopping accounts could be compromised
  • Your identity could be stolen: Hackers with access to your accounts can impersonate you online
  • Your data could be sold: Criminals often sell stolen account information to other bad actors
The threat demonstrates that no computer is completely immune to malware, regardless of the operating system.

What You Can Do

Protect yourself by taking these practical steps:

  • Keep your Mac updated: Install security updates and operating system upgrades as soon as they become available
  • Use reliable antivirus software: Consider installing established security tools designed for macOS
  • Download carefully: Only get programs from official sources like the Mac App Store or verified company websites
  • Enable two-factor authentication: This adds an extra security layer to your important accounts, making them harder to access even if passwords are compromised
  • Log out of websites: When finished with sensitive accounts like email or banking, actively log out instead of letting sessions remain open
  • Watch for unusual activity: Monitor your accounts for suspicious login locations or activities you do not recognize

This emerging threat reminds us that cybersecurity requires constant attention from everyone, regardless of what computer they use.
