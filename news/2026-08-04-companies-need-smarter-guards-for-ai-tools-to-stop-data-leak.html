Companies Need Smarter Guards for AI Tools to Stop Data Leaks

New approach combines existing security tools with AI awareness to prevent employees from accidentally exposing secrets through chatbots.

The Problem Companies Are Racing to Solve

Employees are using artificial intelligence tools like ChatGPT and Claude at work every day—to draft emails, analyze spreadsheets, brainstorm ideas, and solve problems faster than ever before. But here's the catch: many workers don't realize they're feeding sensitive company information into these systems without thinking twice. Customer data, product roadmaps, financial figures, and trade secrets are ending up in AI platforms that companies don't control. Security teams are sounding the alarm.

The real issue is that traditional security tools were built for a different era. They're like security guards trained to stop people at the front door, but they don't understand what happens once employees are inside having conversations with AI assistants. Companies need a new layer of protection that actually watches how employees and AI systems interact with each other.

What This Means

Think of current security as having two separate guards. One guards the building entrance (that's CASB—Cloud Access Security Broker), and another checks what documents people are carrying out (that's DLP—Data Loss Prevention). Both are important, but they miss what's happening in the conversations between employees and AI.

The solution being discussed is adding a third guard who understands the conversation itself. This new layer would:

  • Watch the conversation flow—not just block keywords, but understand context and what information is being shared
  • Spot risky patterns—like when someone repeatedly asks an AI tool questions about confidential projects
  • Prevent AI misuse—ensure the tool itself isn't being manipulated to do something it shouldn't
  • Allow productivity—let employees use AI for legitimate work without constant restrictions

This requires security teams to think differently. Instead of just blocking, they need to understand why employees use AI, what jobs they're trying to accomplish, and where the real risks hide.

Why You Should Care

If you work at any company with valuable information—and that's most companies—this matters to you personally. A single employee accidentally pasting customer names into an AI tool could expose thousands of people's privacy. That employee didn't mean harm; they just didn't think about it.

For business leaders, the stakes are higher. A data breach through AI could damage reputation, invite legal trouble, and cost millions. For security professionals, this is about building trust in AI rather than banning it outright. Employees want to use these tools. The goal is letting them work safely.

What You Can Do

Start by asking yourself and your team three questions:

  • What information is actually sensitive at your company, and who needs to protect it?
  • How are employees currently using AI tools, and what problems are they solving?
  • What guardrails can you put in place that help rather than frustrate workers?

If you're in IT or security, push your organization to evaluate whether your current tools understand AI interactions. If you're an employee, be mindful of what you share with AI assistants—assume anything you paste could be seen by the AI company's systems.

The future of AI at work depends on companies building smart security that enables innovation while actually preventing disasters.
