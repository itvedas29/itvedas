WhatsApp Introduces Username Feature to Shield Your Phone Number from Strangers

WhatsApp is rolling out usernames so people can contact you without knowing your actual phone number, boosting privacy and security.

WhatsApp Adds a New Layer of Privacy Protection

WhatsApp, the messaging platform used by nearly 2 billion people worldwide, is introducing a fresh privacy feature that lets users create custom usernames. This new tool means you can share a simple identifier with others instead of exposing your actual phone number, which is a significant shift in how the app has traditionally worked.

For the first time since its launch, WhatsApp users will have the option to be found and contacted through a username rather than requiring everyone to have their personal mobile number. Think of it like having a nickname for social media that people use instead of your real home address — you maintain privacy while remaining accessible to those you want to communicate with.

Why This Change Matters for Your Security

Your phone number is like a digital skeleton key. Once someone has it, they can potentially:

  • Track your status across multiple apps and services
  • Add you to group chats without your permission
  • Use it for targeted scams or harassment
  • Cross-reference it with other databases to find personal information

The old WhatsApp model required you to share your phone number to stay connected. This left countless people vulnerable to unwanted contact from strangers, data brokers, and bad actors who simply guessed or purchased phone numbers.

By introducing usernames, WhatsApp is finally catching up to competitors like Telegram and Signal, which have offered similar privacy-forward features for years.

What This Means for You

This update addresses a growing problem: phone number harvesting. Scammers and marketing companies compile lists of active phone numbers and use them to launch phishing attacks, spam calls, or impersonation schemes. Usernames create a protective buffer between your real identity and random strangers on the internet.

Additionally, this feature benefits people in vulnerable situations — activists in oppressive regions, domestic abuse survivors, and journalists can now maintain safer communications without broadcasting their phone numbers globally.

For casual users, it simply means cleaner contact management. You can share your username on social media or forums without worrying about every random person having direct access to your personal phone number.

How to Prepare for This Feature

  • Check your WhatsApp settings — Roll out is gradual, so the feature may not appear immediately for everyone
  • Create a username you're comfortable with — Choose something that doesn't reveal personal details
  • Update your privacy settings — Control who can see your profile information when this launches
  • Test with trusted contacts first — Try the feature with friends to understand how it works
  • Review old privacy settings — Ensure your existing phone number sharing preferences are set correctly

What Comes Next

WhatsApp says the rollout will happen gradually over the coming months. Some users may see the feature immediately, while others will receive it later. The company is being cautious to ensure the system works smoothly across different devices and regions.

This development signals that major tech platforms are finally taking user privacy seriously — a positive trend for everyone concerned about their digital footprint.
