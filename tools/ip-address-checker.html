    Quick Answer

    Your public IP address is the address your router or device presents to the wider internet. This page detects your IPv4 and IPv6 address separately (most sites only show whichever one your browser happened to use), plus reverse DNS, the country/region/ISP Cloudflare's network sees, the TLS/connection details of this exact request, and details about your browser — no signup required.

    What is a public IP address?

    A public IP address identifies your connection on the internet, similar to a mailing address. It's assigned by your Internet Service Provider (ISP) and is different from the private IP address (like 192.168.1.5) used inside your home or office network. Websites and services see your public IP, not your private one.

    Why show both IPv4 and IPv6?

    Most networks today are "dual-stack" — your device has both an IPv4 and an IPv6 address, but your browser's Happy Eyeballs algorithm picks whichever one connects fastest (usually IPv6) and never reveals the other on that same connection. To detect both, this tool makes two independent requests: one forced over IPv4-only, one forced over IPv6-only. If your ISP or network doesn't support one of the two protocols, that card will say so instead of showing an address.

    What is reverse DNS?

    Reverse DNS (a PTR record) maps an IP address back to a hostname — the opposite of a normal DNS lookup. Many residential ISPs assign a generic PTR (like 123-45-67-89.static.example.net); some IPs have no PTR record at all, in which case this field shows "Not available."

    How this tool works

    The address you're currently connecting with, plus reverse DNS, geolocation, ASN, and TLS/connection details, come from ITVEDAS's own edge servers reading the connection Cloudflare's network already has for your request — no external service involved for that part. The IPv4/IPv6 dual-detection cards additionally query ipify.org's free, no-logging IPv4-only and IPv6-only endpoints directly from your browser, since forcing a single protocol requires a server that only answers on that protocol — something ITVEDAS's own domain can't do for both at once. Browser details are read directly in your browser via JavaScript and never leave your device.

    Privacy

    Your IP and network details are read from the request itself and returned directly to you. The IPv4/IPv6 dual-detection cards send a request to ipify.org (see above) — no other data is included in those requests. Browser details are read client-side only. Nothing is logged, stored, or shared with third parties by ITVEDAS.

    FAQ

    Why does my IP change?

    Most home internet connections use dynamic IPs that your ISP can reassign periodically, or when you reconnect. Mobile connections and VPNs also change your visible IP frequently.

    Does this show my IPv6 address too?

    Yes — the IPv4 and IPv6 cards at the top detect each address independently, regardless of which protocol your browser actually used to load this page. If a card shows "Not available," your network genuinely doesn't have connectivity over that protocol (common for IPv6 on older ISPs, or for IPv4 on IPv6-only mobile networks).

    Why does the detailed info (ISP, location, reverse DNS) only list one IP?

    Those fields come from the single connection your browser actually made to load this page, so they reflect only that one address — shown in the note above the "Network & Location" section.

    Is my public IP the same as my location?

    Not precisely — geolocation from an IP is approximate and based on ISP registration data, typically accurate to city or region level, not an exact address.

    What's a TLS cipher?

    It's the specific encryption algorithm negotiated between your browser and Cloudflare's edge for this HTTPS connection — a technical detail useful for debugging compatibility issues, not something most visitors need to worry about.
