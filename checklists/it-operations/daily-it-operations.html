Overview

The Daily IT Operations Checklist ensures that critical operational tasks are completed every business day. It covers system monitoring, incident response, security checks, backup verification, and communication tasks essential for maintaining a stable IT environment.

This checklist is designed to take 30-45 minutes and should be performed by the assigned shift operator or IT operations team each morning, afternoon, or during night shifts depending on your schedule.

Who Should Use This Checklist

  • IT Operations Staff
  • NOC (Network Operations Center) Technicians
  • System Administrators
  • Help Desk Managers
  • IT Support Teams
  • Managed Service Providers (MSPs)

When to Use This Checklist

This checklist should be completed:

  • Daily: Every business day at the start of shift
  • During Incidents: Use as reference when troubleshooting critical issues
  • Shift Handoffs: Verify completion during shift transitions
  • Compliance Audits: Document completion for audit trails

Prerequisites

  • Access to monitoring dashboards and alerting systems
  • Credentials for backup verification systems
  • Knowledge of organization's IT incident response procedures
  • Access to change management and ticketing systems
  • Understanding of critical business systems

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping alerts: Don't dismiss warnings without investigating. Alerts often indicate developing problems.
  • Manual verification only: Rely on automated monitoring tools as primary checks, manual verification as confirmation.
  • Incomplete incident documentation: Record all issues, even minor ones, for trend analysis and RCA.
  • Forgetting critical systems: Maintain a prioritized list and use it to prevent gaps in coverage.
  • No handoff documentation: Always pass on relevant information to next shift with specific details.

Best Practices

  • Automate routine checks: Use monitoring tools to alert on thresholds rather than manual review of every metric.
  • Create a runbook: Document exact steps for each critical task so substitutes can follow it precisely.
  • Set up dashboards: Consolidate key metrics into a single view to reduce time spent switching between tools.
  • Establish baselines: Know what "normal" looks like for your environment to spot anomalies quickly.
  • Keep backup contacts: Have escalation numbers and backup personnel readily available.
  • Log everything: Use a consistent format and location for logging results, even successful checks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should this checklist take to complete?
Under normal conditions, 30-45 minutes for an experienced operations technician. Initial implementation may take 60 minutes while familiarizing with systems. During incidents or when issues are discovered, time may extend significantly.
What if a critical system is offline during the checklist?
Immediately escalate to your supervisor or incident manager. Don't wait to complete the rest of the checklist. Initiate incident response procedures and document the issue with timestamp and initial investigation.
Should I check every item even if alerts show no problems?
Yes. Alerts may miss certain issues and confirm no monitoring gaps exist. However, if a system is confirmed healthy by automated monitoring, you can briefly verify rather than exhaustive manual testing.
How do I handle tasks marked "Critical"?
Critical items must be verified every shift without exception. These are typically systems that directly impact business operations. If any critical item cannot be verified, escalate immediately.
What if I need to perform emergency changes during the checklist?
Follow your emergency change procedures, but pause the checklist and note the interruption. Resume after the emergency is resolved and verify systems are still healthy.
Should I modify this checklist for my environment?
Absolutely. This is a template. Remove items not applicable to your environment, add organization-specific systems, and adjust priorities based on your business requirements.
How should I document checklist results?
Create a log sheet or use your ticketing system to record: completion time, who performed it, any issues found, and escalations. Keep logs for compliance and trend analysis.
What's the difference between this and weekly/monthly checklists?
Daily focuses on operational health and immediate issues. Weekly covers more detailed reviews and testing. Monthly addresses strategic items, capacity planning, and compliance verification.
