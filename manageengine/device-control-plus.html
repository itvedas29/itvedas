🔌 Endpoint Security & DLP

ManageEngine Device Control Plus: Stopping Data Walking Out on a USB Drive

Firewalls and endpoint protection don't stop someone from plugging in a USB drive and copying a customer database. Device control tools exist specifically to close that gap — here's how the leading options compare.

Key Facts
  • Device control is a narrower, more focused category than full DLP or full UEM — it's specifically about governing what peripherals (USB, external drives, Bluetooth, printers) can connect and what they can do
  • Policies typically range from full block to read-only to size/file-type-restricted access, applied per user, group, or device class
  • File access auditing (what was actually copied, by whom, when) is often as important as the block/allow decision itself for incident investigation

The Gap Firewalls and Antivirus Don't Cover

Most endpoint security spend goes toward keeping malicious things out — firewalls, antivirus, EDR. Device control solves a different problem: keeping legitimate data from walking out the door on a USB drive, an external hard disk, or over Bluetooth, whether that's a departing employee taking a customer list or an innocent mistake copying sensitive files to an unencrypted personal drive. It's also a common, specific requirement in compliance frameworks (PCI-DSS, HIPAA) that call out removable media as a named risk.

ManageEngine Device Control Plus is a focused tool in this category — it controls and audits USB, external storage, Bluetooth, and other peripheral access, rather than being a full endpoint management or full-DLP suite that happens to include device control as one module among many.

ManageEngine Device Control Plus

Who It's For

IT and security teams that specifically need to control and audit removable-media and peripheral access — often driven by a compliance requirement or a past data-loss incident — without buying a full DLP suite (content inspection, email/cloud-upload scanning) or a full UEM platform they don't otherwise need. If you already run a UEM platform like Endpoint Central with device control as a built-in module, or a full DLP suite, a standalone tool like this may be redundant rather than additive.

How It Compares

ManageEngine Device Control Plus

Focused specifically on peripheral/USB device control: block, allow, or restrict to read-only by device class, user, or group, plus file-copy auditing and file-shadowing (keeping a copy of what was transferred, for investigation). Priced and scoped as a point solution, which makes it a lighter, more affordable pickup than a full DLP suite when device control is genuinely the specific gap you're closing.

Endpoint Protector by CoSoSys

A well-established, cross-platform (Windows/macOS/Linux) device control and DLP vendor with strong content-aware filtering (blocking transfers based on file content, not just device type). More full-featured on the content-inspection side than a device-control-only tool, at a correspondingly higher price and setup complexity.

DriveLock

A European (German) vendor strong in regulated industries, combining device control with broader endpoint hardening (application control, encryption) in one platform. Worth a look if you want device control bundled with wider endpoint-hardening features rather than as a standalone point tool.

Microsoft Defender for Endpoint (device control feature)

If you're already licensed for Defender for Endpoint Plan 2 (via Microsoft 365 E5 or an add-on), its built-in device control policies cover basic USB/removable-media blocking without a separate tool. The tradeoff: less granular policy flexibility and weaker audit/reporting depth than a dedicated device control product, and it only helps if you're already paying for the Defender tier that includes it.
